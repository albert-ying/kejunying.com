AI-Driven Discovery

ClockBase Agent

An autonomous AI system that mines large-scale molecular profiles to discover aging interventions. ClockBase Agent reanalyzed 43,602 intervention-control comparisons across multiple aging biomarkers, identifying 500+ compounds that significantly reduce biological age. Featured on MIT Technology Review.

Species

We expect the longest-lived species to have the most resilient brains. A bowhead whale can live about two centuries and a naked mole-rat outlives other rodents of its size by decades, so we compare their brains with human brains to find protective molecules and cell states.

Population

HealthFlux is a world model built from 502,166 UK Biobank participants. It treats a person’s health as one hidden state that changes between visits and is updated by each new blood test, diagnosis, molecular profile or brain and body MRI. In held-out participants it predicts 1,256 diseases and death over twenty years, and it was tested in four independent US cohorts.

Tissue

We model aging in tissues and organs from the molecules they release and the images they leave. The work includes transcriptomic hallmarks of ageing and mortality shared across more than 25 tissues in four species, plasma-protein models that estimate the age of individual organs, and organ-resolved imaging read by deep learning.

Cell

We study how aging unfolds inside single cells, where coordinated programs and stochastic drift both leave a mark. The work spans single-cell epigenetic aging, the stem and progenitor cells of the long-lived naked mole-rat, clonal B cells that expand with age and drive lymphoma, and foundation models for single-cell data.

Molecule

Proteo-R1 splits protein design into two steps. A multimodal language model reads sequence, structure and text and names the few residues that govern binding. A diffusion model then generates the rest of the sequence and structure with those residues held fixed.

AI agents that do research

Public repositories hold millions of molecular profiles, and most were analyzed once for the question of the study that produced them. Our agents read these data together with the literature, run the analyses and propose what to test. Each result keeps the data, code and papers it came from.

In ClockBase Agent the agents scored public mouse profiles with aging clocks and nominated interventions predicted to lower biological age. In the preprint, one of them, ouabain, slowed frailty progression and reduced neuroinflammation in aged mice.
