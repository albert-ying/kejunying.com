Abstract

DNA methylation-based machine learning models (known as epigenetic clocks) can be used to predict the age of biological samples. Yet, it is unclear whether the clock CpG sites are causal to aging-related phenotypes. Here, we leveraged large-scale genetic data and performed epigenome-wide Mendelian Randomization to identify CpG sites causal to diverse aging-related traits. We show that neither the existing epigenetic clocks nor DNA methylation changes in general are enriched with causal CpG sites. Causal CpGs include an approximately equal number of sites that contribute to aging and protect against it, although their combined contribution negatively affects age-related traits. We constructed a causality-informed aging clock as well as clocks that measure damaging and adaptive changes, which exhibited distinct properties compared to existing clocks. Together, our results offer a comprehensive map of CpG sites causal to lifespan and healthspan, which can be used to build causal biomarkers of aging and assess longevity interventions, age reversal and aging-accelerating events.
