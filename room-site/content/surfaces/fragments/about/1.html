Incoming assistant professor

Hello! I'm Kejun Albert Ying, an incoming assistant professor. I will start my own lab in the Bay Area in January 2027. The exact institution will be announced later.

I am now a postdoctoral fellow at Stanford University in Tony Wyss-Coray's lab and at the University of Washington in David Baker's lab, supported by an NIH/NIA F99K00 fellowship. I earned my Ph.D. in Biological Sciences from Harvard Medical School (2025) in Vadim Gladyshev's lab and an M.Sc. in Computational Science and Engineering from Harvard SEAS (2024), and I received the 2026 WAIC Yunfan Award.

My research focuses on AI for Science, with an emphasis on AI for Biology and AI for Aging. I develop foundation models and world models for biological data, and build agentic systems. I model biological age and discover aging interventions, study brain aging and neurodegeneration, analyze cancer and age-related diseases systematically, and design de novo proteins for aging interventions.

Physics is becoming so unbelievably complex that it is taking longer and longer to train a physicist. It is taking so long, in fact, to train a physicist to the place where he understands the nature of physical problems that he is already too old to solve them.

Solve aging first, and with time, we'll solve all remaining challenges of humanity.

AI-Driven Discovery

ClockBase Agent

Ying, K., Tyshkovskiy, A., Moldakozhayev, A.,..., Wyss-Coray, T., & Gladyshev, V. N. (2025). Autonomous AI Agents Discover Aging Interventions from Millions of Molecular Profiles. bioRxiv. doi:10.1101/2023.02.28.530532

An autonomous AI system that mines large-scale molecular profiles to discover aging interventions. ClockBase Agent reanalyzed 43,602 intervention-control comparisons across multiple aging biomarkers, identifying 500+ compounds that significantly reduce biological age. Featured on MIT Technology Review.
