The shift is striking. Taking the best of the seven frontier models on each task, a zero-shot language model outright beats the entry-level published specialist on 36 of 46 benchmarks (78%, and matches or beats it within 95% confidence intervals on 38 of 46, or 83%). Against the median published specialist across 44 tasks, it outright beats the point estimate on 20 of 44 (45%) and matches or beats it within 95% confidence intervals on 35 of 44 (80%). Evaluated one model at a time, Claude Fable 5.2 alone beats the entry specialist on 33 of 46 benchmarks (72%, median 1.14x of entry and 0.90x of median specialist), and Gemini 4 Neon beats the entry specialist on 26 of 39 text benchmarks (67%, median 1.12x of entry and 0.95x of median). Even the lighter Gemini 3.8 Flash sits at 1.00x of the entry published specialist across 39 text tasks.

All seven frontier models are statistically indistinguishable from PoET 200M, led by Claude Fable 5.2 at 0.938 and Gemini 4 Neon at 0.889.
  • NMR structure elucidation (NMRArena, paired 1H and 13C peak lists): Claude Fable 5.2 hits 85.0% top-1 exact structure accuracy and Gemini 4 Neon hits 75.0%, both ahead of NMRPeak at 50.0% on the exact same 40 molecules (54.3% full split).
  • Small-molecule ADMET (TDC): On Lipophilicity logD, Claude Fable 5.2 (0.385 MAE) and Claude Opus 5.5 (0.448) both beat rank-1 specialist MiniMol (0.456), and all seven models beat DeepPurpose CNN (0.743). On Ames mutagenicity, Gemini 4 Neon reaches 0.888 AUROC against ZairaChem (0.871). On cardiac hERG block, all seven models (0.848 to 0.876) beat both Chemprop (0.721) and the median TDC specialist AttrMasking (0.778), matching MapLight+GNN (0.880).

Only 8 of the 46 benchmarks stay below the entry-level published specialist, and every single one is a dense medical image, multi-channel EEG, satellite image or raw DNA promoter classification task.

3. Two post-cutoff proofs and five channel ablations

Any strong score on public benchmarks raises a fair objection: memorization. Stripping dataset names does not guarantee a model never saw a sequence or reaction during pretraining. Two strictly post-cutoff controls and five paired channel ablations test that directly.

First, on the NEON aquatic ecological forecasting challenge (40 water-temperature forecast windows across 27 sites measured strictly after every model's training cutoff), persistence scores 2.209 CRPS, a 7-day empirical ensemble scores 2.012 and a 30-day linear drift model scores 2.225. Every frontier model beats all three cleanly: Gemini 4 Neon at 1.013, Claude Opus 5.5 at 1.242 and GPT-6.2 Sol at 1.269. Post-cutoff skill matches earlier 2024 to 2026 windows within 0.03 points.

Second, I collected 40 single-chain proteins deposited in the Protein Data Bank between July and November 2026 and asked models to predict long-range C-beta residue contacts (sequence separation at least 24 residues, 11,017 true contacts, base contact rate 1.56%). A sequence-blind rule gets 2.9% precision. On the real post-cutoff chains, all three models output sparse, high-precision contact maps: Claude Opus 5.5 reaches 39.2% precision (217 of 554 predicted pairs correct), Gemini 4 Neon reaches 31.3% (975 of 3,111, F1 0.1405, top-L/5 precision 31.4%) and GPT-6.2 Sol reaches 25.5% (117 of 458). Shuffling each chain internally while keeping length and amino-acid composition collapses all three to chance (1.84%, 2.73% and 1.89%). Stratifying against every pre-July 2026 PDB structure at 80% sequence identity reveals 27 novel chains and 13 close homologs, and every model scores slightly higher on the 27 novel chains (Gemini 0.153 vs 0.115 F1, Claude 0.049 vs 0.030, GPT 0.041 vs 0.028).
